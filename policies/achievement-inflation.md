## Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the responsible inflation of professional achievements on LinkedIn. It exists because "attended a webinar" is not a post and "completed a strategic upskilling initiative in emerging technologies" is not a lie — it is positioning.

This policy applies to all posts describing professional activities, past roles, or anything that happened at work. Read it alongside the [Content Standards Policy](content-standards.html#li-content--the-humble-brag-framework) and [AI Concealment Policy](ai-concealment.html#li-ai--the-authenticity-veneer). For a decision guide before posting an achievement, see the [Should I Post This? process](../processes/should-i-post-this.html).

## Approved Inflation Multipliers

The following substitutions are approved. They represent reasonable reframings rather than fabrications and will survive a follow-up question from a curious connection.

- "I attended a meeting" → "I contributed to a cross-functional alignment session."
- "I read an article" → "I've been exploring the research on..."
- "I watched YouTube videos about AI" → "I've been actively developing my AI literacy."
- "My project was cancelled" → "I navigated an ambiguous and rapidly evolving strategic environment."
- "I sent some emails" → "I led stakeholder communications across a complex initiative."

Multipliers above 3x require supporting evidence. "I single-handedly transformed the company's revenue trajectory" is only permissible if the company has confirmed this in writing and the trajectory moved in your direction.

## The Vagueness Principle

Achievements should be specific enough to sound real and vague enough to be unverifiable. "Increased efficiency by 34%" is too specific unless you can produce the 34% on request. "Significantly improved operational outcomes" is appropriately vague. "Drove meaningful change across the organisation" is vague enough to mean anything and therefore means nothing — approved for general use.

Do not cite specific metrics you cannot defend. Connection requests from people who were at the same company will arrive. They always do.

## Quantification Standards

Where numbers are used, they must be directionally accurate. If the metric improved, the percentage should be positive. If the team grew, the headcount figure should be higher than it was. The specific value is subject to rounding — aggressively.

"I grew the team from 2 to 3 people" may be expressed as "I scaled the team by 50%." Both are true. Only one will impress a recruiter scrolling at speed.

Percentages above 100% draw scrutiny and should be accompanied by context. "We grew revenue by 400%" is memorable; it is also a question waiting to be asked.

## The Led vs Attended Spectrum

The distinction between leading and attending is one of the most consequential decisions in professional self-presentation. The following guidance applies across all posts and profile updates.

- **"Led"** — you made at least one decision that the group accepted.
- **"Drove"** — you advocated for something and it happened, even if others also advocated for it.
- **"Contributed to"** — you were present and said at least one thing that influenced the outcome.
- **"Supported"** — you were present.
- **"Attended"** — this option does not appear in any approved LinkedIn post.
- **"Observed"** — this option does not exist professionally.

Misuse of "led" when "received the calendar invite" is accurate falls under the [AI Concealment Policy risk matrix](ai-concealment.html#li-ai--risk-management) as a separate exposure category. The person who actually led the meeting is also on LinkedIn.

## Certification and Credential Calibration

LinkedIn Learning, Coursera, and similar platforms issue certificates for courses that range from eighteen months to forty-five minutes. This policy does not prohibit sharing these certifications. It prohibits presenting them without appropriate context.

A one-hour certificate may be shared once. It may not be presented alongside a job title, degree, or multi-year credential in a way that implies equivalence. "Certified in Python" following a forty-five minute introductory module is technically accurate and contextually misleading.

The post celebrating a short-form certificate must not: use the word "proud"; use the phrase "I've been working towards this"; suggest the process was rigorous when it was not; or include more than two emojis. A completion badge for a module titled "Introduction to Introduction to AI" does not warrant 🎉🚀🙌💯.

Stackable certificates — completing four one-hour modules from the same provider — do not become a qualification. They become four certificates. They may be posted as one summary update, not four separate announcements. See [Content Standards Policy](content-standards.html#li-content--emoji-governance-framework) for emoji limits applicable to certification posts.
